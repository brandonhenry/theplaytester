# Recruitment, feedback review and outcomes

Start at [My workspace](https://theplaytest.io/#workspace); identify the creator’s exact game. Check it is funded/open and has uncommitted slots before inviting or hiring.

## Find appropriate playtesters

- Review actual applications first. Compare relevant platform access, completed-playtest genre history, previous feedback quality where visible, star rating/count, biography and channel coverage.
- Click the applicant’s image/name to open their profile in a new tab. Profiles can show websites/socials, featured videos and recent channel coverage when available. Interest tags are not proof of prior testing. A high rating with one review is not equivalent evidence to many reviews.
- Use relevant game experience and the owner’s brief to explain a shortlist. Avoid unsupported claims about a person. If the owner authorized selection within stated criteria and limits, proceed; otherwise ask for the selection decision.
- Invitations use the site’s **Invite to playtest** flow for a chosen open owned listing. They invite an application; they do not hire, reserve a slot or guarantee participation. Do not mass-message, scrape contact addresses or advertise elsewhere without authorization.
- **Hire tester** shows a confirmation with the tester, game, reward and deadline. Confirm within the authorized campaign/selection limits, then verify the success state and reserved slot. Re-read if another hire concurrently consumes the last slot.

## Track session states

| State | Meaning / next action |
|---|---|
| `requested` | Application awaiting creator decision; not a funded reservation yet |
| `accepted` | Reward slot reserved; tester normally has seven days to submit |
| `submitted` | Written feedback received; creator normally has seven days to review |
| `changes_requested` | One specific correction requested; seven days to respond |
| `disputed` | Admin review; automatic approval/release paused |
| `completed` | Work approved; transfer and bank payout still have separate statuses |
| `declined`, `cancelled`, `expired` | Not completed; inspect actual reason/reservation/refund status |

Always use the actual `dueAt` or UI deadline. Do not calculate an invented fresh deadline from the time you read a report. Seven-day auto-approval depends on scheduled processing and no timely correction/dispute.

## Review the actual written feedback

Open each submitted playtest. Distinguish:

1. The tester’s private game feedback (built-in yes/no responses, explanations, game-specific answers, overall game impression).
2. Public peer ratings of creator/tester reliability after approved work.
3. Optional publicly available content on the tester’s channels.

A report should summarize specific observations, reproducible bugs and unanswered brief items, with attribution and links to the underlying Firstplay session where the UI provides one. Do not invent deep links: `/#session/...` is not a documented route; open the workspace session.

Current feedback requires five built-in questions: wishlist, play again, clear goals, intuitive controls, and technical reliability. Each asks yes/no followed by at least 30 words in 2–3 sentences. Creator questions follow the same written-explanation rules. Honest “no” answers qualify equally. Required form length is not proof of thoughtful testing; assess the substance against the original brief.

With the owner’s assessment/authority:

- **Approve** complete work. Verify approval success. Explain that an earned reward transfer may still be processing; do not label a failed transfer as a reversed approval.
- Request **one specific correction** tied to missing original-brief work, not new scope or a demand for praise. Resubmission starts a final seven-day review period.
- Raise a **work dispute before the active review/correction deadline** when there is a real unresolved issue. An admin decides. Neither party may unilaterally cancel submitted work.

Do not approve unseen submissions merely to clear a queue. If asked to approve all valid work autonomously, establish the completeness criteria and inspect each submission. Do not turn an “unread feedback report” request into approvals.

## Mandatory peer ratings and rating appeals

Approval creates a mandatory 1–5-star rating obligation for **each** participant. No star is preselected; comments are optional. Ask for the user’s star assessment if they did not give it; do not invent it. The required rating dialog can block starting new work until finished, but does not condition the earned reward. Signing out is possible.

Any creator or tester may dispute a rating they **received**, including an older one: **Profile → Ratings & disputes**, or the rating’s dispute action. Choose a category and give 10–2,000 characters explaining the concern. It remains counted/visible during independent admin review. An admin may keep it or remove it from public reputation with a shared explanation; the original is retained. One case per rating; new evidence after resolution goes through support. This does not change completed work or payments.

Close a campaign report with remaining applicants, occupied/free slots, submitted/approved count, nearest deadlines, disputes, and next decisions. Do not promise a specific number of testers or a turnaround the marketplace has not guaranteed.
